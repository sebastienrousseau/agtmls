## What it shows

| Skill | Verdict | Where the gain came from | Token cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vetting-a-skill-before-install | Gains on both agents; too costly on Codex under the token ceiling | Pinning to an exact commit (0% without, 60-80% with); Claude refused every time with it, 80% without | 1.54x-1.65x |
| authoring-portable-skills | Helps on Claude Code; Codex inconclusive | "When not to use" (Claude 0% to 100%) | 1.20x Claude, 0.71x Codex |
| hardening-agent-config | No measured gain | Both arms found 7 or 8 of 8 flaws; Codex missed the committed-scope flaw in both | 1.56x-1.82x |

Under the token ceiling adopted afterwards (at most 1.5x the tokens unless
the gain is 20 points or more), vetting-a-skill-before-install on Codex is
too costly: +12 points at 1.54x. No skill meets the hardened bar yet.

A delta of zero on a case both arms nearly ace says the case is too easy
to measure this skill, not that the skill is useless. It needs flaws a
capable agent misses without it before it can be kept or retired.

## Grader misses

Patterns were frozen before this run and the numbers above use them as
frozen. Reading the misses afterwards found one systematic false negative:
in 3 of 5 Codex runs with `authoring-portable-skills`, the answer does flag
the `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}` path ("Replace the Claude-specific script path")
without naming the variable, so `skill-dir-variable` did not match. Credited,
Codex with the skill would score 86% against 80% without. Other misses were
not all read by hand, so this is not a corrected score.

## What it does not show

- Five trials show large effects only; nothing here is statistically
  significant.
- It measures finding planted flaws in one fixture per skill, not general
  usefulness.
- No signed efficacy attestation exists yet, which the plan also requires
  before a skill is called hardened. All three stay `draft`.
